\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:literature}

The review starts with speech spoofing benchmarks and moves to all-type detection, compact detectors, robustness to channel changes, consistency regularisation, shortcut learning and model compression. It ends with the gap that this project works on.

\section{From speaker verification to deepfake detection}

Work on fake audio began with attacks on speaker-verification systems. Replay, synthesis and voice conversion led to the ASVspoof challenges and to the EER as the main metric~\cite{kinnunen2017detection,wang2020asvspoof}. ASVspoof~2019 fixed a logical-access protocol for synthetic and converted speech~\cite{wang2020asvspoof}, and ASVspoof~5 later added crowdsourced recordings, adversarial attacks and codec conditions~\cite{wang2025asvspoof5}. Surveys describe how the problem has spread to singing, music and environmental sound~\cite{shaaban2023ieeeAccess,zhang2025sensorsSurvey,alnaqbi2026systematic,nguyenle2026deepfakeSurvey}. They also report that mel-spectrogram CNNs remain a common baseline~\cite{shaaban2023ieeeAccess,mcuba2023deeplearning} and that the most accurate systems use self-supervised encoders, at a much higher computational cost~\cite{li2024sonar}. WaveFake and FakeSound add data for neural vocoders and general audio~\cite{frank2021wavefake,xie2024fakesound}.

\section{All-type benchmarks}

AT-ADD evaluates speech, singing, music and environmental sound together under realistic distortions~\cite{xie2026atadd}. Its Track~2 Macro-F1 averages the real and fake F1 scores inside each type and then averages over the four types, so good speech performance alone is not enough. The leading systems combine self-supervised encoders~\cite{xie2026atadd,fan2026dualssl} or use audio large language models with frequency-time reasoning~\cite{xie2026ftgrpo,gu2025allm4add}. Cross-domain and in-the-wild studies show that detectors lose a lot of accuracy when the recording conditions change~\cite{li2024crossdomain,muller2022generalize}.

\section{Compact detectors and frontends}

Several small detectors exist for speech. Light CNNs work on LFCC features~\cite{lavrentyeva2019stc}, RawNet2 works on the raw waveform~\cite{tak2021rawnet2}, AASIST uses graph attention with a very small parameter count~\cite{jung2022aasist}, and SpecRNet was designed for fast inference~\cite{kawa2022specrnet}. Pham et al.\ fine-tune ImageNet-pretrained vision networks, including MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, on linear-filterbank images~\cite{pham2024deepfake}. A comparison of feature types found that the best choice depends on the network~\cite{mcuba2023deeplearning}. These works use speech data and clean test audio, and they report parameter counts but not measured latency. Table~\ref{tab:related} lists them next to this work.

\section{Robustness to compression, noise and channel change}

Surveys agree that detectors react strongly to codecs, band-limiting, reverberation and noise~\cite{zhang2025sensorsSurvey,alnaqbi2026systematic}. The usual remedy is augmentation. RawBoost applies convolutive and impulsive noise and coloured additive noise to the raw waveform and improves anti-spoofing generalisation~\cite{tak2022rawboost}, and SpecAugment masks regions of the spectrogram~\cite{park2019specaugment}. In the ADD~2022 challenge a fine-tuned wav2vec~2.0 system dealt with low-quality and compressed audio~\cite{martindonas2022vicomtech}. Datasets of perturbed voices and cross-domain collections were built to measure robustness~\cite{gao2024p2v,li2024crossdomain}, and adversarial attacks show further weaknesses~\cite{rabhi2024adversarial}. None of these papers asks whether an explicit agreement loss between a clean clip and its degraded copy helps beyond what augmentation already gives a small network.

\section{Consistency regularisation}

Consistency regularisation penalises differences between a model's outputs for perturbed versions of the same input. It is the basis of temporal ensembling~\cite{laine2017temporal}, unsupervised data augmentation~\cite{xie2020uda} and FixMatch~\cite{sohn2020fixmatch}, and the Jensen--Shannon consistency term of AugMix improves robustness to corruptions~\cite{hendrycks2020augmix}. FT-RCL uses the same idea for supervised detection and differs in two ways: agreement is also required between band-wise internal features, and the weight of the constraint depends on audio type, confidence and severity. Whether these two differences matter is a question for ablation, and the ablation runs are not yet available (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).

\section{Shortcut learning in spoofing corpora}

M\"uller et al.\ found that models trained on ASVspoof~2019 relied on the length of leading and trailing silence and not on spoofing artefacts~\cite{muller2021silver}. Shortcuts of this kind give good in-corpus scores and fail when conditions change. Section~\ref{sec:shortcuts} runs a similar check on AT-ADD.

\section{Efficient inference}

Smaller networks such as MobileNetV3~\cite{howard2019mobilenetv3}, knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} and 8-bit quantisation~\cite{jacob2018quantization} are the usual routes to edge budgets, and pretrained audio networks such as PANNs have compact variants~\cite{kong2020panns}. For deepfake detection these routes are rarely evaluated with measured latency, memory or quantised accuracy.

\section{Research gap}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Compact detectors are almost always tested on speech. Their behaviour on music and environmental sound is largely undocumented.
  \item Robustness is pursued through augmentation, and the added value of agreement losses over an augmented baseline has not been isolated.
  \item Edge suitability is argued from parameter counts, and measured cost is seldom reported.
  \item Validation sets that share generators with the training data hide generalisation failures.
\end{enumerate}
This project looks at the second point for a fixed backbone and, because of the size of the available evidence, mainly provides a careful account of the first, third and fourth.

\chapter{Preliminary Studies}
\label{ch:prelim}

This chapter presents the clean baseline model selection on AT-ADD Track~2 alongside exploratory preliminary observations and architectural context from published literature.

\section{Exploratory frontend observations on ASVspoof 2019 LA}
\label{sec:frontend-study}

During initial exploratory work, preliminary runs examined frontend variations on the ASVspoof~2019 logical-access evaluation list. However, because sampling rate (16\,kHz vs.\ 24\,kHz), duration padding (zero-padding vs.\ repeat-padding), frame length, and hop size were varied simultaneously, no causal attribution can be drawn regarding the isolated effect of any individual factor. Furthermore, because ASVspoof~2019 LA speech is natively sampled at 16\,kHz, up-sampling to 24\,kHz introduces no high-frequency vocoder cues above 8\,kHz. Consequently, these exploratory runs serve solely as historical context and are not presented as experimental evidence of padding or bandwidth superiority. As detailed in Section~\ref{sec:duration-norm}, repeat-padding was selected for the AT-ADD benchmark as a controlled preprocessing choice to prevent deterministic trailing-silence cues across variable-duration recordings, without asserting universal padding superiority.

\section{Clean baseline model selection on AT-ADD Track 2}
\label{sec:backbone-study}

To establish a rigorous clean baseline before evaluating robustness-oriented objectives, three candidate configurations were systematically investigated on AT-ADD Track~2 under the generator-disjoint evaluation protocol across three independent random seeds (seeds 42, 1337, and 2024), structured as a progressive representation study:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{E1 (Compact scratch CNN candidate):} Asks whether a very compact, randomly initialized vision CNN (MobileNetV3-Small, $1{,}519{,}618$ parameters) operating on compact 80-bin log-mel spectrograms can learn the clean AT-ADD multi-type task from scratch.
  \item \textbf{E2 (Temporal recurrence candidate):} Asks whether adding explicit temporal sequence modelling improves upon the compact CNN representation by extending the MobileNetV3-Small features with a one-layer GRU (hidden size 128, $1{,}198{,}082$ parameters) following frequency-axis pooling.
  \item \textbf{E3 (Pretrained audio representation candidate):} Asks whether an AudioSet-pretrained general audio representation provides a stronger clean reference than randomly initialized models. It adapts PANNs MobileNetV2~\cite{kong2020panns} with a newly initialized 80-bin batch-normalisation layer and binary classification head ($3{,}537{,}250$ parameters), leveraging acoustic features pretrained on two million multi-domain clips from AudioSet.
\end{itemize}

The experimental results address the central scientific questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.2em]
  \item \textbf{What did E1 test?} E1 tested whether a compact scratch-trained CNN can discriminate synthetic audio on unseen generators. It achieved meaningful overall separation ($0.8289$ AUROC), but suffered low type-averaged Macro-F1 ($0.5148 \pm 0.0378$; pooled Macro-F1 $0.5634 \pm 0.0130$) due to severe failure on non-speech categories.
  \item \textbf{What did E2 add?} E2 investigated whether explicit temporal sequence modelling improves the compact representation. While E2 improved pooled Macro-F1 ($0.5972 \pm 0.0621$) and slightly reduced EER ($0.2436 \pm 0.0103$), it did not improve type-averaged Macro-F1 ($0.5039 \pm 0.0720$) and exhibited heightened seed-to-seed sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{What did E3 change?} E3 evaluated transfer from a rich, general-purpose audio representation. Unlike E1 and E2, which learned filters from scratch, E3 was initialized with AudioSet-pretrained weights from Kong et al.~\cite{kong2020panns}. While the original PANNs MobileNetV2 uses 64 mel bins and a 527-class head ($4{,}075{,}343$ parameters), our adapted E3 model incorporates a fresh 80-bin batch-normalisation layer and binary classification head ($3{,}537{,}250$ parameters).
  \item \textbf{Which model performed best?} E3 achieved the best results across every clean metric among the tested configurations: type-averaged Macro-F1 was $11.26$ points higher than E1 and $12.35$ points higher than E2; accuracy reached $0.7349$; AUROC reached $0.8800$; and EER dropped to $0.1993$. (Under development-calibrated operating thresholds $\theta^*$, type-averaged Macro-F1 reached $0.6978 \pm 0.0046$).
  \item \textbf{How stable were the models across seeds?} E1 and E2 showed substantial variation across initialisation seeds (E1 AUROC SD was $0.0516$; E2 type-averaged Macro-F1 SD was $0.0720$). E3 showed exceptional stability across runs ($\sigma_{\mathrm{AUROC}} = 0.0032$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{EER}} = 0.0028$), confirming that pretrained audio representations stabilize optimization under severe class imbalance.
  \item \textbf{Why was E3 selected?} E3 proved to be the best-performing tested clean reference model and demonstrated superior reproducibility. While E3 is larger ($3.54$\,M parameters vs $1.52$\,M for E1 and $1.20$\,M for E2), its clean accuracy and cross-seed stability motivated its decisive selection as the official reference baseline \textbf{M0}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Architectural context from published MobileNetV3 systems}

Pham et al.~\cite{pham2024deepfake} demonstrate the viability of lightweight CNN and convolutional-recurrent architectures for audio deepfake detection. Their experiments, however, target ASVspoof~2019 and therefore do not provide a directly comparable numerical reference for the heterogeneous AT-ADD Track~2 setting used here.

Pham et al.\ report an EER of 48\% for an ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3 on three-channel linear-filterbank images of two-second slices of ASVspoof~2019 speech. We re-implemented their signal processing and score aggregation and trained with our own recipe (AdamW, cosine learning-rate decay, class-weighted loss, per-bin normalisation). The utterance-level EER was 16.85\% with AUROC 0.9180 (segment level: 17.01\%, 0.9162). 

This comparison provides architectural literature context for MobileNetV3-based feature extraction (as explored in candidate E1), but must be clearly distinguished from our selected baseline M0 (PANNs MobileNetV2, E3). The published external runs and the AT-ADD benchmark differ simultaneously in frontend, sampling rate, training data, and evaluation protocols, preventing direct apples-to-apples performance comparisons across datasets.
